  "The paper bounds the distance between the law of a normalised sum of \\(n\\) independent random vectors and the Gaussian, uniformly over all convex subsets of \\(\\mathbb{R}^d\\). The upper bounds carry numerical constants, allow arbitrary dependence among coordinates and cover every moment order from three upwards; for identically distributed summands the dependence on \\(n\\), \\(d\\) and the moment bound is shown to be optimal, with matching lower bounds from bounded distributions.",
  "The headline finding is a fourth-moment threshold. Four is the first moment order for which uniform bounds on coordinate moments give a worst-case error linear in the dimension, and bounds on higher moments give the same rate. The proof adds independent Gaussian noise to exploit convexity, proves a normal approximation for Rademacher sums on Parseval frames, and then uses random swaps with independent copies to apply that bound conditionally to general sums."
