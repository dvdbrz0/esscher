  "High-dimensional central limit theorems over hyperrectangles underpin simultaneous inference with many coordinates, and their rates usually require \\(\\log d\\) to grow slowly relative to \\(n\\). Anders Bredahl Kock asks how fast \\(d\\) can grow when only coordinatewise \\(m\\)-th moments are bounded, for a fixed \\(m > 2\\).",
  "He shows that \\(\\log d = o(n^{(m-2)/(3m-2)})\\) is necessary for any statistic, randomised or not, to admit a Gaussian approximation with the covariance or correlation of the data uniformly over the class; the obstruction already appears for coordinate maxima. For \\(m \\in (2, 4]\\), centred and scaled quantile-winsorised means achieve this condition without knowing \\(m\\), so the barrier is sharp; for \\(m > 4\\) they reach it up to a power of \\(\\log n\\)."
