  "Mollick defined artificial general intelligence narrowly, as an AI as good as most human experts at most intellectual tasks done on a computer, excluding physical work and dealings with people. By that standard he judged Fable 5.2 to be almost there, a state he calls jagged AGI: expert-level across many domains, but still well behind people in places that are now harder to describe than hallucinations.",
  "His tests ran across domains. From one prompt the model turned a small USB keyboard into a console with six games, working out the hardware from the code of its control apps. Asked to launch a business, it screened 22 ideas and read public agendas from 201 local governments to build a plan, giving its own estimate of a 20–40% chance of success; Mollick stopped before it went live. It also wrote a research paper on company filing fees that he placed ahead of a second-year doctoral student, though not publishable, and it did not carry out the pre-registration it was asked for.",
  "The gaps, in his account, were of taste: a first course website he called joyless, readings of poems that ignored the author’s own commentary. The model fixed each in minutes once told but noticed none itself. In a thread on X he added that about 25,000 agents building more than 450 linked 3D worlds developed their own bureaucracy, which eventually slowed them down."
