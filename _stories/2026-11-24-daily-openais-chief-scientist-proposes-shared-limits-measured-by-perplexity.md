  "In an essay published on 22 November, Jakub Pachocki, chief scientist at OpenAI, argued that pretraining a better compressor of text is where new capabilities come from, so progress is best tracked by perplexity, equivalently the cross-entropy loss on held-out data. OpenAI’s own figures on its main evaluation set run from a log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 to about 0.91 for GPT-4 and below 0.8 for its newest models, where he says capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics appeared. A model at about 0.76 began producing results on open mathematical problems.",
  "He expects further capabilities, not yet known, in the 0.68 to 0.7 window, and says AI could drive a burst of advances in pretraining research as soon as 2027. He proposes common limits, based on perplexity and other measures, that developers would not cross until a concrete milestone in understanding model behaviour is reached, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. No threshold is specified.",
  "Pachocki lists the problems himself: labs use different datasets, perplexity is not a task, chain-of-thought gains do not show up in it, and the entropy floor of the data is unknown. Sam Altman endorsed the essay on X."
