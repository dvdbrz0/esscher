  "The Commerce Department issued an interim rule on Monday requiring licences to export, re-export or transfer AI chips and servers to any data centre in the world that may provide computing power to companies headquartered in the US D:5 group of embargoed countries, which includes China, Russia, Iran and North Korea. The rule covers indirect access through brokers, and operators must monitor the ownership of their customers.",
  "It applies to facilities able to supply more than 12 million units of total processing performance, the metric used in US export controls, which TechCrunch put at roughly the capacity of 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips. Commercial recommendation systems are exempt. Existing operators have 90 days to obtain licences once the rule takes effect.",
  "The rule also adds licence requirements for advanced chips sent to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, citing a higher risk of diversion, and formally rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025. It follows the resignation of Jensen Huang at Nvidia amid a scandal involving a customer the government suspects of supplying compute to Chinese companies."
