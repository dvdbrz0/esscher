  "François Chollet wrote that every intelligent system he has observed has exactly two timescales of adaptation. The first is modelling a new situation on the fly, which he identifies with fluid intelligence; current AI does this by generating reasoning chains or programs at inference time. The second is extracting reusable abstractions offline from a long buffer of experience, which in current systems is pre-training and post-training.",
  "His claim about history is that modern AI systems had essentially only the second capability until late 2024, when reasoning models arrived. The framing fits his long-standing view that skill acquired from data is different from the ability to adapt to novelty, and it places test-time computation, rather than scale alone, at the centre of recent progress."
